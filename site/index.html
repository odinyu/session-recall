Why you can trust it

Your Mac does the work. Your files stay yours.

The promises are short, so they are written as things you can check. Every statement below describes how the app is actually built.

Search runs on your Mac.

No account, no analytics, no telemetry — your sessions are never uploaded. Search and the reports run in a local process against a local index file, one SQLite database under ~/Library/Application Support/session-recall/. You can watch that file, move it or delete it; the app rebuilds it from your agent history and nothing else.

Your agent files are never touched.

The app opens the session files your agents wrote in order to index them, and it never writes to, moves or deletes them. Starring and hiding change only its own index. Forget removes a session from that index — the original transcript in the agent's own storage stays exactly where it was.

Nothing to sign up for.

No account, no analytics, no telemetry — your sessions are never uploaded. There is no identity in the app at all: no login, no profile, no data tied to you. Delete it and the only trace left is the index file, which you can delete too.

Works out of the box.

Install and open it — no account, no API key, no config file, no cloud service. It indexes the history your agents already wrote on this Mac, so the first search works immediately. The only setup step on a fresh copy is one terminal command to clear the macOS quarantine flag (step 2 of the install below).

How it works

Three steps, then it just works.

Nothing to sign up for and nothing to configure. If your agents have been writing sessions on this Mac, the material is already here.

1. Install.

Download the zip, drag the app to Applications, clear the quarantine flag once. That is the whole setup — there is no account to create and no key to paste.

2. It indexes what is already on disk, read-only.

On first launch the app reads the session files your agents already wrote and builds one searchable index. It keeps the index fresh while it is open, and it never writes to the files it read.

3. Search across every agent from one box.

Type the words you remember — a phrase, a file name, an error line. Results come back from all of your installed agents in one list, each opening at the exact turn, with the resume command ready to copy.

What you get

Two things people actually use it for.

Search is the core; these two are what it feeds. Both come free with the index — no model call, no summary service, no extra setup.

01

Find it by its title.

A session you renamed stays findable: session titles are searched along with the conversation, so a phrase that appears only in the title still brings that session back, marked as a title match in the results. No re-index needed — an existing index finds title matches on the very next query.

02

Search from anywhere, instantly.

Press ⌘⇧K, or summon the window with the global hotkey while it is hidden, and a Spotlight-style overlay opens that searches as you type — ↑↓ to move, Enter to open a session, ⌘↵ to copy its deep link, Esc to close. The whole index in front of you without leaving the app you are in.

03

A report of your own work.

The app writes a daily, weekly and monthly report straight from the index: a per-project table of sessions and turns, the questions you asked in each session, and a resume command for each one, with links back into the app. Read it when you try to remember what you did on Tuesday, hand it to a colleague as context, or see which project ate the week. Weekly and monthly reports compare themselves with the period before — sessions, turns, active projects, per-agent share and the biggest movers — and add rankings of your top projects, agents and busiest hours. Downloadable as Markdown, and it can write them on a local schedule if you want them waiting for you. The same sessions always produce the same report, because it is read out of the index, not summarised by a model. An archive view keeps the past days browsable.

04

Sessions that move between agents.

Find the session in the agent that did the work, then continue it somewhere else: copy the handoff card (project, resume command, recent prompts, the last answer, the files and commands involved), or just the resume command or deep link, and carry on in another agent with the full context. The continue-in picker on a session page lists the agents installed on your Mac. A session id only means something to its own agent — that is why the card travels with you.

05

Keep the good ones, clear the noise.

Star a session and it sits one click away under the sidebar's Starred view — one of three views (All, Starred, Hidden), each with a live count. Hide tucks a session out of the default view, with Undo right in the status line. Saved searches keep a filter combination by name: save it from the rail, restore the whole view in one click. Forget removes a session from the index — and, as above, never from your agent's own storage.

06

Copy the answer, skip the noise.

Every turn carries copy question, copy answer and copy Q&A, and the whole conversation copies as Markdown in order, with a 200 KB cap that notes where it truncated. Paste it into a document, a review, or another agent.

07

Summon it from anywhere.

A global shortcut, ⌥⌘R by default, brings the window up over whatever you are doing; press it again to hide it. Rebind it or switch it off in settings — ⌘W hides the window, ⌘Q quits cleanly, and clicking the Dock icon brings it back.

08

Updates without the download ritual.

When a new version exists, the app tells you: a dialog shows the version, its release notes and a changelog link, and one click installs it — no manual download, no re-clearing the quarantine flag.

Install

Two minutes to your first hit.

  1. 01

    Download, unzip, drag to Applications.

    The Apple silicon and Intel builds are the same app. Once unzipped it is about 75 MB and carries its own runtime, so there is nothing to install first.

  2. 02

    Clear the quarantine flag.

    Session Recall is ad-hoc signed, not notarized: notarization needs a paid Apple Developer account. So macOS blocks the first launch and says the app is damaged. Run this once per copy and it opens normally afterwards: 

    xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine \
  "/Applications/Session Recall.app"
open -a "Session Recall"
# from a clone, for the CLI and MCP server
bun run scan
bun run sr "websocket" --limit 3
bun run mcp
  3. 03

    Open it and search.

    First launch reads the sessions your agents already wrote and builds the index. Type the words you remember — quote them if you want the exact phrase. There is nothing else to set up.

macOS 11 or later. The app carries its own runtime; Bun 1.3+ is only needed for the CLI, the MCP server, or a build from source.
Index: ~/Library/Application Support/session-recall/index.db

For people who script things

A bonus, not a requirement.

Everything above works in the app alone. If you also live in the terminal, the same index is available two ways: bun run sr "…" searches it from the shell, and bun run mcp serves it to your coding agent over the MCP protocol — your assistant can then look up past sessions itself, read-only, on this Mac.

MCP server docs 
{
  "mcpServers": {
    "session-recall": {
      "command": "bun",
      "args": ["run", "/absolute/path/to/session-recall/src/cli.ts", "mcp"]
    }
  }
}

Questions

What people ask first.

Does anything leave my Mac?
No account, no analytics, no telemetry — your sessions are never uploaded. Search and the reports run in a local process against a local index file.
Which agents does it index?
Eight: omp, pi, OpenCode, Cursor, Alma, Cindy, Claude Code and Codex. Any other agent can be added with two lines of JSON, so if you use several of them, this is the one place that searches across all of them at once. Every one of them is read read-only, and each excludes its agent's private records.
Does it modify my history?
No. Your agents' session files are opened read-only and never written to, moved or deleted. The only file the app writes is its own index, and deleting that index costs you nothing but the time to rebuild it.
Why does macOS warn on first launch?
The app is ad-hoc signed rather than notarized, so Gatekeeper treats a fresh copy as untrusted and reports it as damaged. Clearing the quarantine attribute once per copy tells macOS to trust it from then on: xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine "/Applications/Session Recall.app"
Apple silicon or Intel?
The Apple silicon build is 27.2 MB and the Intel build is 29.9 MB. Both are v1.9.2 and both need macOS 11 or later. If you are not sure which Mac you have, open the Apple menu and choose “About This Mac”.
What about Windows and Linux?
No. Session Recall is a macOS app and reads agent storage from macOS locations. The CLI and the MCP server run under Bun, but there is no Windows or Linux build.
How current is the index?
The app scans on boot and refreshes every 90 seconds while it is open. The MCP server refreshes in the background once the index is more than five minutes old, and reports its age with every reply.
Is there a global shortcut?
Yes. ⌥⌘R summons the window over whatever you are working on; press it again to hide it. In the app's settings you can rebind it to another combination or disable it entirely, and a change applies after a restart. One macOS quirk: after a rebinding, the old registration can keep working too, so both combinations may summon the app until you restart.
What does hiding a session do?
Hiding tucks a session out of the default search and the sidebar views without deleting anything. The daily and weekly reports skip hidden sessions, but a deep link still opens one, and a handoff by session id still works — hiding is a filter, not a lock. Unhide from the session page, or find it again under the sidebar's Hidden view.
How do I update?
You usually do nothing: when a new version appears, the app shows a dialog with its release notes and an Update now button — one click installs it, and the index outside the app is reused untouched. The manual way still works: download the newer zip from Releases, replace the app in Applications, and clear the quarantine flag on the new copy.
